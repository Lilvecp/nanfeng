# Business model · B2B2B (r1-05)

**One line:** banks and trade-finance fintechs license NanFeng as a white-label CFO for their SME exporters; the SME gets the agent for free, the lender gets better-timed, better-understood short-term credit.

## Who pays and why

| Actor | What they get | What they pay |
|---|---|---|
| **Lender** (bank, factoring company, trade-finance fintech) — the customer | Requests for financing that arrive *before* the crunch, with a 90-day probabilistic forecast, the reason for the need and an explainable Credit Passport; cheaper origination and early-warning on its portfolio | License per active SME + success fee on financing originated through NanFeng |
| **SME exporter** — the user | Forecast, cheapest-financing plan, collections and approvals on WhatsApp, voice and web | Nothing (bundled by its bank) |
| **Importer** (e.g. in Mainland China) | Reminders and payment confirmations in its own language and channel (WeCom on the roadmap) | Nothing |

Why B2B2B and not direct to SMEs: SMEs don't buy software for cash management, but they already have a bank. Lenders already pay to acquire and underwrite SME credit; NanFeng lowers both costs. This is the language of the WeBank jury: **cash flow as alternative credit data**.

## Neutrality

NanFeng shows every eligible product, including competitors of the licensing bank, ranked by the optimizer. The licensing bank gets the first right to quote, never a hidden thumb on the scale: the Financing Arena shows the cost of every option and why each one is in or out. Without this the SME stops trusting the plan.

## Revenue mechanics (pricing hypotheses, to validate with lenders)

| Stream | Hypothesis | Illustration with the Cherry Crisis |
|---|---|---|
| License | Flat monthly fee per active SME | — |
| Success fee | Basis points on the principal originated through an approved NanFeng plan | PAE loan of CLP 140.1M: each 10 bps = CLP 140,100 |
| Data / risk API | Credit Passport and early-warning signals on the lender's existing portfolio, with the SME's consent | Passport 34 (E) → 67 (B) after the plan |

All prices above are **assumptions**; the deck must say so. The only measured numbers are the SME-side savings (−45% in the Cherry Crisis, −41% average on NanFeng-Bench).

## Go-to-market

1. **First corridor:** Chile → Mainland China fresh fruit (cherries are ~87% of a USD 2.8 bn season to China). One Chilean bank or factoring fintech as design partner.
2. **Same corridor, other side:** Chinese importers and their banks (WeCom channel, CNY reminders).
3. **APEC:** the problem — exporters paid late and financed expensively — is the same in every APEC economy. A new corridor is a new channel adapter and a new product catalog, not a new agent.

## Risks and answers

| Question from the jury | Answer |
|---|---|
| Does the bank want to show cheaper competitors? | Neutral ranking is the product; the bank wins on first right to quote and on data, not on hiding options. |
| Who is liable if the plan is wrong? | The agent never executes L3 without the owner's OTP; the lender still underwrites; every step is in an audit log anchored on-chain. |
| Is this only for Chile? | No: Chile → China is the first corridor; catalog and channels are per country. |
